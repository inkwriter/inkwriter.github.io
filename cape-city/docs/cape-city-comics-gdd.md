# CAPE CITY COMICS
### *Every Run Is an Issue.*

A 32-bit top-down superhero roguelite where your powers evolve, the city is destructible, and villains remember every fight.

**Working title rationale:** The game frames itself as a comic anthology. Every run is an "issue" — the run-start screen is a comic cover (procedurally generated with your hero's name, costume colors, and the featured nemesis), and the run-end screen is the back cover with your story recapped in panels. This framing turns permadeath into a feature: your hero's run *was* a complete comic issue, and the next run is Issue #2 with a new hero in the same living city.

Alternate titles if this one doesn't land: **KAPOW COUNTY**, **MERIDIAN KNIGHTS**, **THE DAILY CAPE**, **HERO PRESS**.

---

## 1. Core Design Pillars

1. **Every hero feels different.** Power slots + random offers + d20 events mean no two builds converge.
2. **The city remembers.** Districts track your deeds; civilians react; reputation is a style, not a score.
3. **Villains evolve and adapt.** Beat a villain with ice, they come back with heat coils. They gain XP when you don't.
4. **Destruction is fun but risky.** Everything breakable is a weapon and a liability.
5. **Every run creates a comic-book story.** The game records your run as issue panels and headlines.

---

## 2. Core Gameplay Loop

**Moment-to-moment (seconds):**
Patrol district → event pops (crime/disaster/rescue) → engage with powers → environment reacts (destruction, hazards, chain reactions) → civilians flee/cheer → resolve → XP + reputation shift.

**Session loop (minutes):**
Choose district from city map → complete 2–4 events → mid-mission d20 triggers possible → nemesis may ambush → return to base (Hero HQ) → spend Power XP, pick upgrades → newspaper headline recaps your choices → next district.

**Run loop (hours):**
Origin → build power kit across 20–30 levels → villain network escalates ranks 1→5 → nemesis rivalry arc (2–4 encounters, each adapted) → Mastermind finale → issue back-cover recap → meta-unlocks carry to next run (new starting powers, costume parts, city lore).

**Failure loop:**
Defeat ≠ game over immediately. First defeat by a nemesis: they gain rank and brag; you wake up in HQ with a grudge marker. Second defeat in the same run: issue ends ("TO BE CONTINUED...?"), meta-progress banked.

## 3. Game Modes

### 3.1 Origin Mode
Pick 1 of 8 starting Core Powers: Super Strength, Super Speed, Flight, Ice, Electricity, Tech Armor, Energy Blasts, Elastic Body. Pick a costume palette. Standard difficulty curve.

### 3.2 Random Origin Mode
The game rolls: starting power (any of 30), a weakness (from the tradeoff table), a personality trait (affects dialogue bubbles + one gameplay quirk), and a starting district. Bonus meta-currency for finishing a random origin run.

**Personality traits (examples):**
- **Showboat** — +Rep from crowds, −Rep when no witnesses see your rescue.
- **By-the-Book** — bonus XP for zero collateral, penalty for property damage.
- **Hothead** — +10% damage, taunting villains raises their grudge faster.
- **Softie** — pets and civilians rescued give double XP; can't intimidate villains.
- **Tinkerer** — starts with 1 gadget; finds parts in rubble.

### 3.3 Evolution Mode
Start as an ordinary citizen (delivery courier, lab intern, teacher — pick a day job that sets your starting district). No powers, only a stamina bar and a bicycle. Powers arrive via story events: the science accident, the strange meteor, the artifact in the museum. Slowest start, biggest d20 exposure — Evolution Mode heroes trigger d20 rolls ~40% more often. The power fantasy of going from "person with a fire extinguisher" to "person who IS a fire extinguisher" is the point.

## 4. Player Progression

### 4.1 Four XP Tracks
| Track | Earned by | Spent on / causes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hero XP** | Everything | Character levels → new power offers every 5 levels, +HP/stamina |
| **Power XP** | Using that specific power | Levels that power 1→5 |
| **Reputation XP** | How you resolve events (not just that you did) | Shifts reputation style per district |
| **Villain XP** | Villains earn it when they escape, win, or complete crimes off-screen | Villain rank-ups, adaptations, new minions |

### 4.2 Power Slot System
| Stage | Core | Support | Passive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early (Lv 1–9) | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Mid (Lv 10–19) | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| Late (Lv 20+) | 1 | 3 | 2 |

- New power offers arrive at levels 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 — choose 1 of 3 random options.
- If slots are full, taking a new power means **benching** one (benched powers keep their Power XP and can be re-slotted at HQ between districts).
- Ultimate Powers occupy no slot — they're rare rewards (d20 rolls, Mastermind fights, perfect-rescue streaks) with long cooldowns.

### 4.3 Universal Upgrade Shape
Every power follows the same rhythm so players can read any power at a glance:
- **Lv 1: Basic use**
- **Lv 2: Combat upgrade**
- **Lv 3: Mobility or utility upgrade**
- **Lv 4: Advanced technique**
- **Lv 5: Signature move**

## 6. The d20 Event System

Certain major events trigger a visible on-screen d20 roll (chunky pixel die, comic "RATTLE RATTLE" text). The roll is a *moment* — the game pauses, crowd watches, die tumbles.

**Triggers:**
- Defeating a District Boss or higher
- Surviving a science accident
- Saving 5+ civilians from one disaster
- Destroying a villain lab
- Touching a strange artifact
- Being defeated by your nemesis
- Perfect rescue (zero collateral damage)
- Ending a city-wide emergency

**The table:**

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Unstable Mutation.** Gain a random new power — with a temporary drawback for 3 missions (e.g., gain Electricity, but water hazards deal double damage to you). |
| 2–4 | **Power Surge.** One equipped power acts +1 level for the next mission. |
| 5–8 | **Insight.** Big Power XP drop for the power you used most in the triggering event. |
| 9–12 | **Breakthrough Option.** Unlock an upgrade choice you didn't have yet. |
| 13–16 | **Passive Perk.** Gain a permanent minor passive (from a 12-perk pool: +stamina, faster rescue interactions, cheaper repairs, etc.). |
| 17–19 | **Rare Technique.** Choose 1 of 2 rare variant upgrades (e.g., Piercing Bolt vs. Ricochet Bolt for Energy Blasts). |
| **20** | **Heroic Breakthrough.** Gain a new power *starting at Lv2*, or instantly level an equipped power. Confetti. Newspaper front page. |

**Design note:** Even a roll of 1 gives the player something — the drawback creates story, not punishment. The d20 is the game's randomness *celebration*, so every face should feel like a comic plot twist.

## 8. Destructible Environment Rules

**Tile classes:**
| Class | Examples | Breaks under | Consequence weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Props | benches, signs, hydrants, trash cans | any hit | ~0 (hydrants make fun geysers) |
| Vehicles | cars, vans, boats | strength/explosive/repeat | Low (empty) / HIGH (occupied — always shows "!" if occupied) |
| Structure-light | fences, doors, windows, storefronts | most powers | Low–Med |
| Structure-heavy | walls, small buildings, bridges | Lv4+ powers only | HIGH — repair costs, rep damage |
| Anchors | clock tower, museum, landmarks per district | scripted events only | Story consequences |

**Systemic interactions (the Streets of Rogue heart):**
- Fire spreads along wooden props; ice and water stop it; wind (flight) pushes it.
- Electricity conducts through water puddles, metal fences, and rails.
- Broken hydrants create puddles (electricity synergy/danger) and douse fires.
- Frozen enemies shatter cover when thrown; ice paths make civilians comically slip (no damage, big rep hit if they were elderly).
- Thrown cars explode into foam-and-spring cartoon parts, never fire, unless a fire villain ignites them.
- Rubble from destroyed walls becomes throwable/liftable objects (and Tech Genius parts).

**Collateral damage meter:** per-mission tracker. Feeds Reputation XP and repair costs. Crushed prop = 1 pt; wall = 10; small building = 50; occupied vehicle endangered = 25 + instant rep event.

## 9. Reputation System

Reputation is a **style vector**, not a bar. Two axes tracked per district and citywide:
- **Effectiveness** (crimes stopped, civilians saved)
- **Care** (collateral, civilian fear, cleanliness of wins)

| Style | Effectiveness | Care | City behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beloved Hero** | High | High | Discounts at HQ, civilians assist (point at clues, cheer = stamina regen) |
| **Feared Vigilante** | High | Low | Street criminals flee on sight (fewer small crimes, less XP), civilians keep distance |
| **Chaotic Hero** | High | Mixed | Crowds love you, city council doesn't; repair costs up, fan mail gives random buffs |
| **Neighborhood Legend** | High in 1–2 districts | High there | That district: free rooftop fast-travel, informant tips, unique missions |
| **Reckless Menace** | Any | Very low | A rival hero begins hunting you; missions to rebuild trust appear |

Reputation changes come with newspaper headlines between districts ("CAPE OR CALAMITY?") — the paper is the reputation UI.

## 10. Nemesis-Style Villain System

### 10.1 Villain generation
Each villain is assembled from parts:

`Name = [Adjective/Noun] + [Theme noun]` → *"Marquis Magnet," "The Garden Gnome," "Coach Chaos," "Puzzler Prime," "Sir Slapstick."*

**Attributes:** costume palette + silhouette parts, power/weapon kit (from villain-safe pools), personality (Braggart, Coward, Planner, Prankster, Honorable, Dramatic), weakness (element, ego, specific gadget, loyalty to minions), grudge score, memory log, rank (1–5), controlled district, relationships (fears/respects/rivals other villains), and an **adaptation list**.

### 10.2 Memory & dialogue
Villains keep a memory log of every encounter: how they lost, what power beat them, what you destroyed, whether you spared their minions. Speech bubbles reference it:
> "Last time you froze my hoverboard. Notice anything DIFFERENT about my NEW hoverboard, hero?!"

### 10.3 Adaptation table (excerpt)
| You beat them with... | They return with... |
|---|---|
| Ice | Heated armor (ice slows 50% less) |
| Thrown objects | Catch-and-return skill (telegraphs it — you can fake them out) |
| Invisibility | Motion-sensor goggles + flour bombs that reveal you |
| Flight | Net cannons, rooftop turrets |
| Electricity | Insulated rubber suit (looks ridiculous; they're defensive about it) |
| Ranged spam | Mirror shield minion |
| Heavy collateral | They fight in crowded areas ON PURPOSE and call the news |
| Silent takedowns of minions | Buddy-system minions (pairs, one always watching) |

Each villain holds max 2 adaptations — gaining a third replaces the oldest (they can't counter everything; build-switching beats them).

### 10.4 Ranks & Villain XP
1. **Street Troublemaker** → 2. **Powered Criminal** → 3. **District Boss** → 4. **City Threat** → 5. **Mastermind**

Villains earn Villain XP by: defeating you (+++), escaping a fight (+), completing an off-screen crime while you were in another district (+), humiliating you publicly (++, triggers their brag broadcast). Rank-ups change their silhouette (bigger cape, more minions, lair upgrade).

### 10.5 The Nemesis
The first villain who *defeats you* (or escapes you twice) is flagged **Nemesis**: guaranteed 2–4 more encounters per run, fastest adapter, gets the run's finale if they reach rank 4+. Defeating your nemesis for good triggers a d20 roll and the issue's climax panel.

### 10.6 Example villains
- **The Garden Gnome** (Plant/Prankster, Powered Criminal) — tiny, rides a lawnmower mech, weakness: compliments about his garden.
- **Coach Chaos** (Sports, District Boss of Entertainment) — dodgeball barrages, whistle stuns, minions run drills. Honorable: won't attack during rescues.
- **Marquis Magnet** (Element, City Threat, Industrial) — steals your thrown objects mid-air; weakness: wooden props.
- **Static Cling** (Speed+Electric, Street Troublemaker) — annoying, escapes constantly, will absolutely become your nemesis.
- **Puzzler Prime** (Puzzle, Mastermind candidate) — deathtrap-free "brain traps"; defeating him requires solving one mid-fight.
- **Foreman Wreck** (Construction, District Boss) — crane hooks, wrecking ball; ironically hates YOUR collateral damage and gains rep when you cause it.

| Underground Transit | tunnels, trains | darkness (light powers shine), no flight | runaway train |

Each district tracks its own reputation, villain influence %, and repair state. A district at 100% villain influence spawns a District Boss siege event.

## 12. Mission Catalog

**Combat:** stop robbery • bank heist • villain hideout raid • nemesis ambush • rival hero challenge • District Boss siege
**Rescue:** building fire • lift rubble • catch falling civilians • runaway train • bridge collapse • pet rescue • escort
**Chase:** getaway vehicle • rooftop pursuit • track villain signal
**Investigation:** follow footprints (Animal Instincts synergy) • examine clues • find the hidden lab
**Weird:** calm malfunctioning robot • defuse cartoon gadget (wire-cutting minigame) • prevent science accident • stop villain TV broadcast • parade protection

Missions chain: a robbery might reveal a clue → hideout → lab → boss. Chains build the run's "issue plot."

|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | LMB | Ice Shot | Punch |
| Special | RMB | Freeze Cone | Throw Object |

Readability rules: enemy attacks always telegraph (flash + comic "!" bubble); friendly fire never harms civilians directly, but *environmental consequences* of your powers can endanger them (the interesting kind of danger).

### Weakness/tradeoff table
| Power | Drawback |
|---|---|
| Super Strength | everything you touch breaks; +collateral |
| Super Speed | drifty indoors; knock over props at speed |
| Fire | panics crowds, spreads via props |
| Flight | useless in Transit; wind pushes you in storms |
| Invisibility | energy drain while moving |
| Tech Armor/Genius | needs parts from rubble |
| Healing Factor | low burst damage budget |
| Gravity | huge stamina costs |
| Electricity | self-risk near water |
| Ice | slippery hazards for civilians |
| Sonic | shatters every window nearby |
| Size (Grow) | can't enter buildings; steps break sidewalk |

## 14. UI

- **Comic cover run-start** and **back-cover recap** (auto-generated panels from run stats).
- **Newspaper between districts** — headlines are the reputation/nemesis feed.
- **HUD:** HP hearts (chunky), stamina lightning pips, power buttons with cooldown sweeps, combo button glows when available, collateral meter as a small "$" ticker, mini-map with civilian "!" pings.

- **Villain intro cards:** freeze-frame, name plate, rank stars, "LAST TIME:" memory line.
- **Impact words** (KRAK! ZAP! WHAM!) as sprite bursts on hits — biggest hits get screen-edge panel borders for one beat.

## 15. Art Direction

House style (matching Beastmarch / Dragonseal / Marigold Lane lineage), pushed brighter:
- 16×16 base tiles rendered at 3× (48px), chunky outlines, 32-bit-era palette depth.
- **Palette:** saturated primaries + halftone-dot shading on large surfaces for the comic feel (2-color dither = cheap halftone).
- Characters ~16×24 with 4-direction facing; capes get a 2-frame flutter.
- Ben-Day dot skies, thick panel-border screen shake on ultimates.
- Speech bubbles: white, black 2px border, pixel tail; villain bubbles get jagged borders.
- Civilians: 8 palette-swapped bodies × 4 heads = crowd variety cheap.
- NO gore, NO occult imagery: defeated enemies sit down with stars circling; robots pop springs; "arrested" = bubble-wrapped by police.

## 18. Keeping v0.1 Simple but Expandable

1. **Powers as data + verbs.** Engine implements ~8 verbs (dash, projectile, melee, zone, shield, lift, tether, buff); every power is a data recipe combining verbs with stats/palettes. 30 powers becomes content, not code.
2. **One district template, many skins.** Citygen reads a district config (tile weights, event table, palette). Downtown MVP proves it; districts 2–10 are config files.
3. **Adaptation = tag counter.** Log damage-type tags the villain received; adaptation just equips a counter-tag. No AI cleverness needed for it to *feel* clever — the speech bubble does the storytelling.
4. **Reputation as one axis first.** The 5-style system is two axes; ship one, add the second when districts exist.
5. **Deterministic seeds.** Seed the RNG per run so bugs are reproducible and players can share "issue seeds."
6. **Comic framing is cheap polish.** Cover/newspaper are DOM+CSS over the canvas — high identity value, near-zero engine cost.
7. **No save system in MVP** — runs are 20–30 min. Add localStorage meta-progress in v0.2. *(Note: in-Claude artifact versions keep state in memory only; saves land when hosted on itch.io.)*

**Suggested phases:**
- **v0.1 (Issue #0):** MVP above.
- **v0.2:** combos, 3 more districts, villain relationships, meta-unlocks, saves.
- **v0.3:** Evolution + Random Origin modes, rival heroes, ultimates, full 30-power roster.
- **v0.4:** Mastermind finale structure, anchor-building story events, issue-seed sharing → itch.io release under Inkwriter Studios.
